About Sushi Love

A sushi bar that behaves like a neighborhood restaurant.

The kitchen makes one promise and repeats it: the freshest sushi on the Boulevard, with real flavor and presentation worth putting your phone down for.

At the table that means fish sliced when you order it, rice seasoned the same morning, and rolls that arrive looking like somebody cared — because somebody did. Sit at the bar and watch it happen. Ask what came in today; the answer changes.

But it's the other half of the menu that turns first-timers into regulars. Homemade ramen. Teriyaki off the grill. Korean bulgogi, crisp katsu, fried rice, udon, and a run of starters — gyoza, spring rolls, stuffed jalapeños — that vanish before the sushi lands. Nobody at your table has to eat raw fish to eat well here. That's the whole point.

The rooms are small, warm and unfussy. Booths you settle into. Servers who know the menu and don't hover. Free parking a few steps from the door. Come on a Tuesday with the kids, or bring six people on a Friday and order the Fire Roll just to watch the next table turn around.
