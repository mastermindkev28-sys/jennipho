scales: one full platter, one close macro of a single nigiri, one steaming bowl,
one hand-and-chopstick action shot, one of the room. Natural light near a window
for the food, tungsten for the room. Avoid stock photography — guests can tell,
and it's the fastest way to look like every other sushi website.

## Photography shot list — one afternoon, whole site

Shoot in this order and you'll have everything above in about three hours:

1. **Fire Roll, flamed** — the hero. Lights down, shoot burst, pick the tallest flame.
2. **Four signature dishes, overhead** — identical angle and surface, back to back.
3. **Sashimi Deluxe platter** — the gallery hero. Slightly high, 30° off axis.
4. **A single nigiri, macro** — close enough to see the rice grains.
5. **Ramen bowl with steam** — backlight it so the steam reads.
6. **Chef's hands at the bar, mid-slice** — motion is what makes a food site feel alive.
7. **A full table, candid** — real guests, permission asked, no one facing camera.
8. **The room, warm and occupied** — the About section shot.
9. **Both exteriors at dusk, sign lit** — one per location.
10. **A tied takeout bag on the counter** — for the ordering section.

Shoot everything in landscape *and* square. Modern site layouts need both, and
re-cropping a landscape photo into a square almost always loses the plate.
